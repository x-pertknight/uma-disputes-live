Most challenges end with the oracle saying "too early, ask again."

How a challenge works

Someone proposes the result of a market and puts money behind it. If nobody objects, that result stands. Anyone who disagrees can challenge it, also with money down. The challenge then goes to a vote of UMA token holders, which takes at least two days.

What the past year shows

4,423 challenges were settled on Polygon. More than half came back "too early." About one in five changed the answer. The rest confirmed it.

The first challenge doesn't decide

On Polymarket, the first challenge to a question sends it back to be asked again, whatever the vote says. Only a later challenge can put the market to the vote. Nine in ten challenges are first ones. Among the later ones, about two in three confirmed the original answer.

The rise after June

Among challenges that got a verdict, 61% overturned the answer after June, up from 36%. Counting all settled challenges, it is 26%, up from 18%. Much of the rise matches a change in what gets challenged: sports betting lines went from 13% of the cases to 42%, and they are overturned about 70% of the time. Month by month the match is loose.

How long it takes

A typical challenge settles in three to three and a half days. The slowest that settled took about 20. Three are still open after more than a week, the oldest since 9 December 2025.

Limits

People choose which results to challenge, so these numbers show how often challengers win. They do not show how often proposed results are wrong. Counts are of challenges, not markets. The "ask again" rule is read from the contract code rather than traced market by market.
